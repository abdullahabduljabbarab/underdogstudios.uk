The round

HUD and radar

Built and playtested

The HUD (heads-up display: the gauges and panels drawn over the game) tells each player what they need while driving fast. Its centre is the radar, a round holographic map that turns with the car, routes the courier to the job and decides what the police know about the van. The radar matters more than any other screen element, because in an asymmetric chase what each side can see is the game.

How heat works on the radar

"Heat" is how much the police know about the van. It depends on the cargo, which Abdullah designed with three tiers:

CargoWhat the police radar showsWhat the courier sees
LegalNothingNearby police, as always
IllegalA search circle every 6 s, never centred on the van, shrinking from 60 m to 25 mThe rim flashes red on each ping
HotA pulsing blip that updates each second, 1.5 s behindThe same rim warning
Any, when seenA pink-red diamond, then a fading "last seen" crossA spotted warning, a HUD hook for Ieuan

The van and every clue to it are hot pink-red. Abdullah's reason: yellow is neutral and does not say "catch him", and plain red would be lost among the cops' red and blue.

From the build

Each radar stage was captured in the game at the same spot, from the old camera radar to the final one. Abdullah drove it after every pass. Heat was tested with two instances: a server as the courier and a client as a cop, with the van moved out of sight on the server. Abdullah then played it with three players and called sightings and last seen "much better now".

Behind the design

Why hud, radar

Built 22 Oct to 30 Oct 2026 · Who Abdullah (code, radar design, testing), Ieuan (HUD widgets and radar frame)

Inspired by

GTA V (Rockstar North, 2013): the radar sits in a corner, turns so the player always points up, and draws a GPS route along the streets to the next goal.

What we took: the turning radar, the route along the roads and the zoom with speed. What we changed: a hologram look, and the police radar never shows the van live. It shows only search circles and late sightings, so hiding is a real escape.

The Gamer's Brain (Hodent, 2017): a player's attention is limited, so an interface should show what matters now and make the most important thing stand out.

What we took: each readability pass removed detail until only routes and blips had strong colour. What we changed: warnings moved off the radar and onto the HUD, where Ieuan can style them with the rest.

What we set out to do

Ieuan owns the UI and builds it in Blueprint (Unreal's visual scripting), so the first goal was to let him build every panel with no C++ and no programmer time. The second goal came from Abdullah: a radar that looks and works like GTA's. Ieuan agreed to hand the radar over to him. The prototype's radar was a live camera above each car, which was costly and hard to read.

The big decisions

Full comparison: Trade-off Appendix.

OptionProsConsVerdict
A map of the city built at round start, drawn flatCosts almost nothing per frame; can be styled freelyA builder to writeChosen
A camera rendering the city (the prototype)ExactDrew the city again every frame on every car; hard to readRejected

Measuring the city showed it was drawn twice a frame, once for the screen and once for the radar camera (see the City chapter). The built map removed that cost and freed the look. Buildings are coloured by "family" (the pack's building types) because the pack's real roofs are all grey concrete. Abdullah felt a grey map was "soulless" for a colourful game.

OptionProsConsVerdict
Police never see the van live; sightings 1 s lateBreaking line of sight is a real escapeCops must work togetherChosen
Live while any cop can see it (150 m)Easy for the policeThe van was almost always shown; hiding was pointlessRejected

Abdullah noticed in his test that the police could see the van nearly all the time. The sight range was 150 m, but the police radar only shows 55 to 85 m round the car. Any van on the radar was almost always in some cop's sight. Now a cop sees the van within 80 m in a 100 degree cone in front, or 40 m all round. The team gets a sighting that moves every 1.5 s and sits 1 s behind.

The sweep fix is worth explaining. The radar material worked the angle out as "time since the start, divided by the time for one turn". Nitro shortens the turn from 3.2 s to 1.4 s. Every small change therefore moved the angle by an amount based on the whole match so far, and the longer the match, the worse the jump.

Problems and fixes

The alley change was Abdullah's question: was there a better way that Dafydd could work with while building the level? A hand-placed path is exact, and moving a building never changes it. To judge it, Abdullah had a proper test alley cut through one block of the sandbox city.

Lessons

  • Look at a HUD element in the game at the size players see it, not on a test image.
  • Never multiply a clock by a speed that changes; move the position on each frame instead.
  • When the level designer knows the answer, let them mark it rather than guessing from the geometry.
