The round

Parcels and jobs

Built and playtested

What it does for play. Parcels are the reason the van is on the road. Each job is one pickup and one drop-off. The type of parcel decides what it pays and how loudly the van shows up on police radar, so every job is a choice about risk.

How it works

RuleWhat happens
One job at a timeA new job appears after each delivery, marked on the radar with a route to follow.
Parcel typesLegal or Illegal, from a mix that gets riskier as the round goes on. Never illegal on the first job.
Hot parcelsAn illegal job can turn Hot: it pays double, and the police see a live blip.
LoadingStop in the bay, slow enough, for a moment. The parcel flies into the van.
DeliveringStop in the drop-off bay. The parcel flies out, the van is paid and earns progress towards a star.
Leaving earlyDriving off a bay mid-load does not wipe it: the progress drains slowly, so a van chased off can come back.
DitchingHold the ditch key to throw the parcel out. No pay, the heat stays for a while, and the next job comes later.
Cargo weightIllegal cargo makes the van a little heavier.
PoliceThe police never see the job, the bays or the parcel. Only the cargo type reaches the heat system.

Behind the design

Why parcels

Built by Abdullah · Numbers by Dafydd · In-game HUD by Ieuan · Tested by The team (readability)

Inspired by

Crazy Taxi (Hitmaker, 1999): fares are colour-coded by distance before you pick them up, and an arrow points the way so the player never stops to read a map.

What we took: reading a job at a glance and never leaving the driving. What we changed: the colour also tells the courier how much the police will know.

Adams and Dormans (2012): a game's economy works through sources and drains that players learn to read. Fixed pay rules per parcel type keep that source readable.

Why we chose this

Pre-production planned two offers after every delivery, one legal and one illegal, picked by steering into one. When parcels were built, Abdullah saw the flaw: a courier could take safe legal jobs forever. He wanted every game to play differently, so the game now rolls the parcel type itself.

OptionForAgainstVerdict
Two offers, pick by steeringA clear choice every jobThe courier could farm safe jobs foreverReplaced
Fully random pointsEndless varietyJobs 50 m apart or in dead ends; hard to balanceRejected
One job at a time, mix rises over the roundSimple to read; the round gets riskier on its ownThe courier no longer picks the risk directlyChosen

The choice of risk moved from picking a job to how the courier drives it: which route, whether to ditch, and when to head for an exit.

What can go wrong

SituationWhat the game does
Too few bays fit the distance bandThe band widens step by step, then falls back to any bay, so a job always exists
A cop parks in a bayBays ignore police cars, so cops cannot steal or block jobs
The van ditches mid-chaseThe heat stays for 10 s, so ditching is an escape but not a free one
A repair garage visit with illegal cargoThe garage refuses: a garage cannot hide a van carrying contraband
